How to play

Every shot, choose where to hit, then where to move for the reply. Your opponent moved somewhere after their last shot. Hit where they are not.

The court has a forehand side, a middle, and a backhand side. Guess the right side and you get an easier ball. Cover the middle and you are never badly beaten, but you rarely read a shot. Guess the wrong side and you are stretched, and a strong shot is a winner.

Crosscourt is safe. Down the line changes direction, so it pushes harder but misses more. Forehands hit harder than backhands. When a ball comes to your backhand and you are balanced, you can run around it for an inside-out or inside-in forehand, but it leaves your forehand side open.

Depth and spin matter. Deep balls pin you back. Short balls let you attack. Low slice is hard to attack and hard to volley. Heavy topspin to the backhand is awkward. A short angle pulls your opponent off the court, so the next ball into the open court is a winner, but it sits up if they get there in time.

Serving: you alternate deuce and ad courts. The returner picks where they think the serve is going and how far back to stand. Flat serves are big but miss often, slice curves away and pulls the returner wide on the deuce side, and kick is safe and jumps up high. After the serve, stay back and cover a side, or serve and volley.
